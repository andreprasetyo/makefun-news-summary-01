World news, 11 November 2026: Russia, Ukraine and the US began a second day of peace talks in Abu Dhabi, their first direct talks since February, with territory the main obstacle (Reuters). UN chief António Guterres told COP31 leaders in Antalya the 'climate crisis is outrunning us'; nearly 200 countries began leaders' speeches, the US absent again (Reuters). US prosecutors charged three men over an alleged Iran-backed campaign of nearly 20 attacks, including a foiled September plot at RAF Fairford; Iran denies involvement (AFP). The US House voted 211-204 to refer eight impeachment articles against Defence Secretary Pete Hegseth to committee, ending the bid this session (NPR). Congo's Ebola outbreak passed 11,042 cases and 5,387 deaths, its deadliest ever and the second-largest on record, with no approved vaccine or treatment (AP). Mississippi's Senate race stayed uncalled, with Democrat Scott Colom ahead of Republican Cindy Hyde-Smith by about 2,600 votes, 97% counted (AP). Nvidia hired Wachtell Lipton to investigate CEO Jensen Huang over his admitted affair with a woman US officials suspect of spying for China, as shares lost about $430bn in two sessions (BBC). US prices rose 0.4% in October, above forecasts, lifting bets on a December Fed rate rise; Asian shares climbed, Kospi up 2.6%, Nikkei up 1.3% (AP). El Salvador's president said his troops and Haitian police destroyed the headquarters of Haiti's 400 Mawozo gang; reports of 20 killed are unconfirmed (BBC). A Florida judge let President Trump's $10bn defamation case against the BBC proceed over an edited January 6 clip; the BBC denies defamation (Reuters). British police arrested a 41-year-old US Army officer near Gloucester on suspicion of spying for Russia; he has not been charged (BBC). A bus crashed into 32 vehicles in Seongnam, South Korea, injuring 57 people, nine seriously; the driver said the brakes had failed (Reuters).
